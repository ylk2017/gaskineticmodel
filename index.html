Try these investigations

  • Boyle’s law: keep voltage and bead count fixed. Double the load with external pressure off: volume halves.
  • Charles’s law: keep load, area, and bead count fixed. Double the voltage: temperature and volume double.
  • Avogadro’s law: keep pressure and voltage fixed. Double the bead count: volume doubles.
  • Area: with external pressure off, increasing area lowers pressure and increases volume, while piston height stays unchanged.
Model assumptions and limits
  • The piston is frictionless. Its mass is included in the total load.
  • Each control change immediately establishes a new equilibrium. Piston transients and heat-transfer times are not modeled.
  • The cylinder has no height limit in this idealized model.
  • Beads are point-like ideal-gas particles; bead–bead interactions and excluded volume are neglected.
  • Particle speeds follow the √T dependence. Motion is slowed for visibility; particles use a reference molar mass of 28.97 g/mol.
  • Pressure is calculated from the ideal gas law, not estimated from the finite number of displayed collisions.
  • The base vibration is symbolic. The imposed relation T = αU supplies the temperature calibration.
  • With external pressure off, P = mg/A. With atmosphere on, absolute pressure is P = Patm + mg/A.
