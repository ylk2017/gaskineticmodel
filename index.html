Physics model

Bead–piston collisions transfer momentum and change the piston velocity. Gravity pulls the piston downward. The ideal gas law does not set the piston height.

The base is a thermal wall representing the vibrator. Outgoing bead velocities are sampled from the temperature selected by the voltage.

Side-wall collisions are elastic. Bead–bead interactions and bead gravity are omitted. The displayed beads are point-like; their drawn sizes do not affect collisions.

External pressure is zero. Therefore mg/A is the equilibrium gas pressure when the piston is not resting on the bottom stop.

The fixed guide lines are visual references, not an upper stop. If the piston leaves the view, zoom out manually.

Teaching experiments and calibration
  • Increase voltage: the base heats the beads. Stronger collisions tend to raise the piston.
  • Increase load: the piston initially accelerates downward. Compression increases collision frequency.
  • Add beads: more impacts tend to raise the piston. The base width does not change.
  • After settling, compare measured collision pressure with mg/A. These need not match during piston acceleration.

Mechanical calibration: bead mass = 0.001 kg; kmodel = 0.0001 J/K per bead; base temperature = 50 K/V. This effective energy constant replaces molecular kB for the macroscopic analogue.

For ideal-gas bookkeeping, one bead can represent kmodel/kB ≈ 7.24 × 10¹⁸ molecules. Its mechanical mass is chosen independently for visible motion. Voltage-to-temperature calibration is an imposed analogy, not a universal relation for real vibrators.

The simulation uses small discrete timesteps and approximate contact-position corrections. It is a classroom model, not a precision molecular-dynamics solver.
